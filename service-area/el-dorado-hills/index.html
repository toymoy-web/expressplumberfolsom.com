El Dorado Hills has grown fast, and its housing stock reflects that: large master-planned communities like Serrano, built around its own country club and golf course, and The Promontory, a gated community along Folsom Lake's shoreline, sit alongside older sections closer to the El Dorado Hills Town Center on Highway 50. That range matters for plumbing the same way it does in Folsom itself, newer slab-foundation construction has different failure patterns than anything built even a decade or two earlier.

Homes in the Serrano and Promontory area are almost universally slab-foundation construction with copper or PEX supply lines, and El Dorado County's rolling, oak-studded terrain means more grading and fill work went into many of these lots than a flat build site would require. Fill soil settles differently than undisturbed ground, and those transition zones under a foundation are a real, checkable contributor to slab leaks over time, not a hypothetical risk.

Because El Dorado Hills sits at a higher elevation than Folsom itself, water pressure and delivery can behave a little differently depending on which pressure zone a specific street sits in, something worth mentioning if you've noticed pressure that seems inconsistent with what neighbors describe. We check static pressure as a matter of course on any call here rather than assuming Folsom-typical numbers apply directly across the county line.

We dispatch to El Dorado Hills for the same range of work we handle in Folsom: water heater installation and repair, drain cleaning, leak detection, repiping, and full emergency response for burst pipes and active leaks. Being just up the highway means a real arrival window rather than a vague same-day promise routed through a call center that doesn't know the difference between Serrano and the Town Center.

El Dorado Hills also sees a fair number of homes with pools, spas, and irrigation systems tied into the main water line, similar to what we see in Folsom's own lake-adjacent neighborhoods. A leak in any of those systems can run for weeks without an obvious sign beyond a creeping water bill, and by the time it's noticed visually, it's often already affecting landscaping or, in the worst cases, foundation moisture. If your bill has climbed without a clear explanation, that's worth a real leak-detection visit rather than assuming it will resolve on its own.

The El Dorado Hills Town Center area, closer to Highway 50, tends to have a different mix of commercial and multi-family plumbing needs than the residential-heavy neighborhoods further into the hills. We handle both, and if you're a property manager or business owner in the Town Center corridor, the same commercial plumbing standards we apply in Folsom, backflow testing, grease trap service, minimal-downtime scheduling, apply here as well.

El Dorado Hills sits entirely within the single 95762 ZIP, a real distinction from a multi-ZIP city like Rancho Cordova, and every address in that ZIP, from Serrano down to the streets nearest the Folsom line at 95630, gets the same 24/7 emergency dispatch and the same water heater and leak detection response we run in Folsom itself.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

  • Locate and shut off your home's main water valve before anything else
  • If the leak is near an electrical outlet or panel, avoid that area entirely
  • Move furniture and valuables away from the water if it's safe
  • Call us with your street and nearest cross-street so we can route efficiently
  • Don't wait to see if it slows down on its own, call while it's active
