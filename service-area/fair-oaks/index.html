Fair Oaks is a community of about 32,500 people in Sacramento County. It is bordered by Orangevale to the east, Citrus Heights to the north, Carmichael to the west, and the American River to the south. That puts it directly beyond Orangevale on the same side of the river we already cover from Folsom, so arrival windows stay realistic. Madison Avenue and Fair Oaks Boulevard are the main routes in.

Fair Oaks began as the Sunset Colony, part of the old Rancho San Juan land grant, and was first surveyed in 1895. A water company and a fruit company followed within a few years. Because the community grew in stages over more than a century, one street can mix older homes, mid-century additions, and newer infill. Each era brings its own pipe materials, and we check for that before we quote.

The heart of town is Fair Oaks Village, built around Plaza Park, Village Park, and the community clubhouse on Fair Oaks Boulevard. The Fair Oaks Historical Society keeps its History Center at 10340 Fair Oaks Boulevard, and the Village hosts the autumn Chicken Festival. Sailor Bar Park and Phoenix Park run along the river side of town. We serve homes and the small businesses around the Village.

Fair Oaks is known for rolling, tree-canopied streets. Mature trees are a gift, but their roots go looking for water, and older sewer laterals with joints and seams are easy targets. Repeat main-line clogs are the pattern we see most in neighborhoods like this. A camera inspection shows where roots are getting in before anyone digs, which keeps repairs small and targeted.

About seven in ten occupied homes in Fair Oaks are owner-occupied, and the typical resident is older than the California median. In practice that means long-held homes where original supply lines, aging water heaters, and old fixtures come up often. We check pipe age and material early, so you hear about a weak line before it turns into a flooded floor.

Homes closer to the American River can see more groundwater during a wet winter. That makes sump pumps and crawlspace drainage worth checking before the rain starts. If you have had water in a low spot before, tell us when you call and we will look at drainage as part of the visit.

We dispatch to Fair Oaks for the same work we do in Folsom: water heater repair and replacement, drain cleaning, hydro jetting, sewer line repair, leak detection, and repiping. Fair Oaks is served by ZIP code 95628, and our 24/7 emergency line covers all of it. Every repair follows California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards, with permits pulled where required.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

  • Close the main water valve first, usually where the line enters the house or at the street meter box
  • Shut off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water
  • Lift rugs and move small furniture out of the wet area to slow the damage
  • Take quick photos for your insurance claim before you start cleaning up
  • Call our 24/7 line and give us a cross street near Fair Oaks Boulevard or Madison Avenue
