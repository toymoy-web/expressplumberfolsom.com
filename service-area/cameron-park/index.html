Cameron Park is a foothill community of about 18,900 people in El Dorado County. From Folsom it is a straight run east on Highway 50, roughly 12 to 15 driving miles, just beyond El Dorado Hills. It is a census-designated place rather than a city, so county rules and county permitting offices apply instead of a city hall.

Modern Cameron Park began in the 1950s, when Larry Cameron bought about 5,000 acres of foothill land. He used it first for ranching and then for housing, a golf course, parks, a lake, and a small airport. That one development shaped much of today's neighborhoods, so many homes share an age range and building style, and that helps us predict what we will find.

The best-known landmark is Cameron Park Lake, which the Community Services District stocks with fish and where the annual Summer Spectacular is held around Independence Day. Next to it sit the Cameron Airpark Estates, where wide streets double as taxiways leading to the airport's 4,051-foot runway. Many Airpark homes have backyard hangars, so we ask about every building on the property when we plan a leak or water heater job.

Cameron Park sits in the Sierra Nevada foothills at a higher elevation than Folsom, on lots that are larger and more varied than a flat subdivision. On sloped ground, water pressure can differ from one street to the next, and long supply runs to detached buildings are common. We check static pressure when we arrive, and we confirm early whether a property is on public water and sewer or on a well and septic system, because that changes the repair.

Cameron Park's ZIP code is 95682, shared with Shingle Springs, Diamond Springs, and a small part of El Dorado Hills. Because one ZIP covers several communities, we confirm your street address rather than relying on the ZIP alone. Neighboring Shingle Springs, which shares that ZIP, is covered from this same page.

Homes in a foothill community like this tend to run their water heaters and pressure regulators hard. A failing regulator can push pressure high enough to stress every fixture in the house, and it often shows up first as a dripping relief valve or a noisy pipe. If you hear banging when a faucet closes, mention it when you call, because it points to pressure rather than a single bad fixture.

We dispatch to Cameron Park for water heater repair and replacement, leak detection, drain cleaning, burst pipe repair, septic tank pumping, and repiping. Our 24/7 emergency line covers the whole community. Every repair follows California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards, with El Dorado County permits pulled where required.

If You Have A Burst Pipe Or Active Leak

  • Shut the main valve at once, or the well pump switch if your property runs on a well
  • Keep away from standing water near outlets and let us handle anything electrical
  • Clear the wet area of rugs, boxes, and small furniture to limit the damage
  • Take photos of the leak and any damage for your insurance claim
  • Call our 24/7 line and tell us whether you are near Cameron Park Drive or the Airpark side
