Empire Ranch is one of Folsom's large master-planned communities. Its developer describes about 1,738 acres of rolling hills with oak groves, parks, trails, schools and a golf course, planned for more than 3,000 families. Listing sites show homes built from around 2000 onward, with a mix of single-family and attached homes.

Those listing figures are aggregated from third-party data and are not verified, so we do not treat them as facts about your house. What we can say is that larger homes tend to have more bathrooms, more fixtures and longer pipe runs. That means more places to check when a leak or a pressure problem shows up.

Water heaters in bigger households

A busy family home asks a lot from its water heater. If hot water runs out quickly, comes back slowly or the tank is making noise, it is worth having it checked before it fails. Our water heater repair and water heater replacement pages explain the options, including tankless units for homes that need steady hot water.

California requires water heaters to be braced or strapped under Health and Safety Code section 19211, so a replacement should include proper strapping. SMUD has also offered rebates of up to $4,000 on heat pump water heaters for SMUD-metered homes, with conditions such as a permitted project. Check SMUD's current rules before you buy, because programs change.

Leaks that hide in larger homes

With more rooms and more runs of pipe, a small leak can go unnoticed for weeks. Warning signs include a water meter that keeps moving when everything is off, a bill that climbs without explanation, or a warm spot on the floor. The City of Folsom says meters are usually near a side property line by the sidewalk, in a concrete box, and that its DropCountr portal shows your usage history.

If your meter moves with every fixture off, our leak detection crew can trace the leak electronically. Pools, spas and irrigation tied into the main line are also worth including in the check.

Pressure, noise and banging pipes

A knocking sound when a faucet shuts or the washer stops is often called water hammer, and it is worth checking because it stresses fittings over time. Low pressure at one fixture usually points to a local clog or a worn part, while low pressure everywhere points to something at the main. Tell us which you are seeing when you call so we arrive prepared.

Fixtures and everyday repairs

Not every call is an emergency. Running toilets, dripping faucets and a garbage disposal that hums but does not spin are all common. Fixing them early protects the water bill and avoids a bigger repair later. Our toilet repair and faucet repair pages cover what we handle.

Common questions

Is my Empire Ranch water heater required to be strapped?

California law requires water heaters to be braced or strapped. If yours is not, mention it when you call and we can include it with a repair or replacement.

How do I check my meter for a leak?

Turn off every fixture, then look at the meter. The City of Folsom says meters are typically near a side property line by the sidewalk, in a concrete box. If the dial or flow indicator keeps moving, there may be a leak on your side.

Do you handle homes with pools and irrigation?

Yes. A leak on a line feeding a pool, spa or irrigation system can raise a water bill quietly. We can include those lines in a leak check.
