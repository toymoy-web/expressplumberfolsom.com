Sump Pump Installation & Repair in Folsom, CA

A sump pump that fails during a storm is a problem you find out about the hard way. We install, repair, and battery-back-up pumps before that happens.

A sump pump's job is simple but critical: it collects groundwater or runoff that accumulates in a low-lying crawlspace, basement, or sump pit and moves it away from the house before it becomes standing water. Homes in parts of Folsom close to the American River and Lake Natoma, and any home with a crawlspace lower than the surrounding grade, are more likely to need one, since groundwater during the wet winter months finds the lowest available point.

The most common failure mode we see isn't a dramatic pump breakdown, it's a pump that's been silently not working for a while and nobody knew until water actually showed up. A pump can fail from a stuck float switch that never triggers it to turn on, a clogged intake screen restricting flow, or a worn impeller that no longer moves water effectively. Simple old age matters too, most sump pumps have a useful life of 7 to 10 years before the motor starts to go.

A battery backup system is worth serious consideration if your sump pump matters at all, because the situations that cause sump pumps to be needed most, heavy winter storms, are exactly the situations most likely to also cause a power outage. A standard AC-powered pump with no backup is completely useless during the exact event it exists to handle if the power goes out at the same time, which is a real and recurring pattern, not a rare edge case.

For new installations, we size the pump to the actual water volume the space needs to handle, not just whichever unit is cheapest. We route the discharge line to daylight or an appropriate drainage point well away from the foundation, since a poorly routed line can redirect water back toward the house instead of away from it. We test every new installation with an actual water fill to confirm the float switch and pump cycle correctly before considering the job done.

If your home has experienced water intrusion in a crawlspace or low area and doesn't currently have a sump pump at all, that's worth a conversation before the next wet season rather than after the next flooded crawlspace. And if water has already caused damage before a pump was in place or working, that cleanup and any resulting mold concern is handled separately from the pump installation itself, a distinct restoration scope rather than part of the plumbing fix. We're happy to point you toward the right resource for that side of the job as well, so you're not left sorting out restoration on your own after a plumbing fix.

Signs your sump pump needs attention

  • Pump hasn't been tested or run in over a year
  • No battery backup, and the area matters if the power goes out
  • Unusual grinding or rattling noise when the pump runs
  • Water in a crawlspace or low area despite having a pump installed
  • Pump is original to the house and over 8 years old
Quick test: pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the pump activates and drains it within a minute or two. If it doesn't turn on at all, that's worth an immediate call.

Before you call

Common questions

Do I actually need a sump pump in Folsom?

If your home has a crawlspace lower than the surrounding grade, or you're near the American River or Lake Natoma, it's worth having one. We can assess your specific property.

Why do I need a battery backup if I already have a pump?

Because power outages and heavy storms tend to happen together, exactly when a sump pump is needed most. A standard pump with no backup does nothing during an outage.

How long do sump pumps typically last?

Usually 7 to 10 years before the motor starts to fail. Testing it annually catches problems before they cause actual water intrusion.

Can you add a sump pump to a crawlspace that's never had one?

Yes, we assess the space, size the pump appropriately, and route the discharge line to a proper drainage point away from the foundation.
