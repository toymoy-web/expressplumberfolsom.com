A water heater rarely fails on a convenient schedule. Most calls we get start the same way: a puddle at the base of the tank, rusty water at the tap, or a pilot light that won't hold. If your unit is between eight and twelve years old and any of that sounds familiar, it's usually cheaper in the long run to replace it now than to keep patching a tank that's already corroding from the inside.

Folsom's housing stock spans a wide range of ages, and that affects what a water heater swap actually involves. Historic Folsom homes near Sutter Street often still run 40-gallon atmospheric-vent tanks installed decades ago in a garage or a tight interior closet. Bringing that setup up to current California Plumbing Code sometimes means updating the venting, the seismic strapping, or the expansion tank, not just swapping the unit itself. Newer construction out toward Empire Ranch and Broadstone is more likely to already have code-compliant venting and a drain pan, which keeps the swap itself faster.

We install both standard tank units and tankless systems, and we'll tell you honestly which one fits your situation instead of upselling the more expensive option by default. A tank replacement is usually the right call for a household on a budget or with straightforward hot water needs. A tankless system makes more sense if you're frequently running out of hot water with multiple people showering back to back, or if you're renovating anyway and have room in the budget for the upgraded gas line or electrical work tankless units sometimes require.

Sacramento County's water carries enough mineral content that scale buildup inside a tank is a real factor in how long a unit lasts, faster than in areas with naturally softer water. That's part of why a tank that looks fine on the outside can already be failing inside. We check the anode rod and sediment buildup during any service call, even ones that started as something unrelated like a slow drain elsewhere in the house.

Every installation includes a permit pulled through the City of Folsom where required, correct sizing based on your household's fixture count and peak usage, and a haul-away of the old unit. We don't leave a leaking or condemned tank sitting in your garage. If a water heater failure has already caused water damage to flooring or drywall, that's a separate scope of work, water damage restoration and mitigation, from the plumbing repair itself. We'll be upfront about which part of the job we handle directly and which needs a specialist.

If you're comparing tank and tankless side by side, our tankless water heater service page covers the maintenance a tankless system needs that a tank never did, worth reading before you decide which way to go.

Signs your water heater needs attention

  • Rust-colored water at hot taps, or a metallic smell
  • Popping or rumbling sounds from the tank (sediment buildup)
  • Water pooling at the base of the unit
  • Pilot light won't stay lit, or the unit is over 10 years old
  • Hot water runs out noticeably faster than it used to

Before you call

Common questions

Tank or tankless: which is right for a Folsom home?

Depends on household size and budget. Tank units cost less upfront and are simpler to install in an existing setup. Tankless units take up less space and never run out of hot water, but usually need a larger gas line or dedicated electrical circuit. We'll walk through both during the quote.

Do I need a permit to replace a water heater in Folsom?

Yes, in most cases. We pull the permit as part of the job so the work is documented and inspected.

How long does a replacement take?

A straightforward tank swap is usually done same-day. A tankless conversion or a job that needs venting or electrical updates can take longer, we'll give you a real estimate before starting.

What size water heater do I need?

It depends on the number of bathrooms, household size, and simultaneous hot water demand. We size it on-site rather than guessing from a chart.
