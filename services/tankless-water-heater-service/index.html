Tankless Water Heater Service in Folsom, CA

Tankless units need a maintenance step tank heaters don't: a periodic flush. We flush, service, and repair tankless systems across Folsom so yours keeps performing the way it did on day one.

A tankless water heater is a different machine than the tank it replaced, and it has a different failure pattern. Instead of slowly corroding from the inside like a tank, a tankless unit's heat exchanger can gradually collect sediment and mineral deposits from the water passing through it. Folsom's city water is soft, so that buildup is slower here than in many areas. It still happens, and skipping the flush is a common reason a tankless unit starts underperforming years before it should.

The symptoms are usually gradual rather than sudden. Water that used to hit full temperature in a few seconds now takes noticeably longer, the unit cycles on and off instead of holding a steady temperature, or you start hearing a delayed 'clunk' before hot water arrives. None of that means the unit is failing outright, but it does mean the heat exchanger is working harder than it should, and that shortens the unit's useful life if it goes unaddressed.

A proper descaling flush involves isolating the unit with its service valves, circulating a descaling solution through the heat exchanger for a set period, and flushing it clean, not just running vinegar through a garden hose. Manufacturers set a maintenance interval based on water hardness, and soft water like Folsom's generally allows a longer interval than hard water does. Many tankless warranties still require documented maintenance to stay valid. If you bought your unit with the house and don't know when it was last serviced, that's worth checking before it becomes a warranty dispute down the line.

Beyond descaling, we handle tankless-specific repairs: ignition failures, error codes on the unit's display, inadequate gas supply causing the unit to derate itself, and venting issues specific to how tankless units exhaust. These units run cooler and wetter than a standard tank's flue, which means the venting material has to be rated for it. If your tankless unit is throwing an error code, that code is almost always the fastest way to diagnose the problem, tell us the code when you call and we can often narrow down the likely cause before we're on-site.

If you're weighing whether to switch from a tank to tankless during a broader renovation, it's worth knowing that heat pump water heaters are a real, growing alternative worth a look too, particularly if your utility offers incentives for electrification. We'll talk through all three options honestly rather than steering you toward whichever is most profitable for us to install.

And if you're not sure whether your existing unit is worth flushing versus replacing outright, our water heater installation and replacement page walks through when a full swap makes more financial sense than continued maintenance on an aging unit.

Flushing and descaling: what the visit covers

Flush and descale calls are some of the most common tankless requests. We isolate the unit, circulate a descaling solution through the heat exchanger, rinse it, and check the inlet filter and the temperature output. Most homeowners are asking whether it is worth doing and how often.

Folsom's water is soft, so scale builds more slowly here than in hard water areas. A regular flush still clears sediment and gives us a chance to catch small issues early. We recommend a schedule based on how heavily the unit is used.

Repairs, error codes, and when to replace

Tankless units tell you what is wrong through error codes, but the code does not always point to the part. Ignition problems, flow sensor faults, and vent issues are common. Bring the model number and the code when you call and we can usually say whether it is a service call or a bigger problem.

If a unit is old and the repair cost is high, we will tell you honestly whether to repair or replace. We never push replacement on a unit that has more life in it.

Signs your tankless unit needs service

  • Hot water takes longer to arrive than it used to
  • Water temperature fluctuates or the unit short-cycles
  • An error code showing on the unit's display panel
  • It's been longer than your manufacturer's flush interval since the last service
  • A rattling or clunking sound before hot water starts flowing
Have your error code ready when you call. It's printed on the unit's display and speeds up the diagnosis before we even arrive.

Common questions

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed?

Manufacturers set the interval by water hardness, and Folsom's city water is soft, so the interval is often longer here than in hard-water areas. We check your unit's manual and its condition, then tell you honestly whether it is due.

What does an error code on my tankless unit mean?

It's a manufacturer-specific diagnostic code, tell us the code and the brand when you call and we can often identify the likely issue before arriving.

Can a tankless unit run out of hot water?

Not the way a tank can, but it can be undersized for simultaneous demand (two showers plus a dishwasher running at once), which shows up as reduced flow or temperature rather than running cold outright.

Does skipping maintenance void my warranty?

Often, yes. Most tankless manufacturers require documented annual maintenance for the warranty to remain valid. We can provide service documentation for your records.
