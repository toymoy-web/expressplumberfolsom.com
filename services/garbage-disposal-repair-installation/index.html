Garbage Disposal Repair & Installation in Folsom, CA

A jammed or humming disposal usually isn't a full replacement job. We clear the jam, check the motor, and only recommend a new unit when the old one's actually done.

A disposal that hums but won't spin is almost always jammed rather than dead. Every disposal has a manual reset button on the underside of the unit. Most jams clear with the built-in hex-key wrench socket, also on the underside, used to manually rotate the flywheel and free whatever's stuck, usually a fruit pit, bone fragment, or fibrous material like celery or corn husks wrapped around the blades. We check this first on every call before assuming the motor itself has failed.

A disposal that's completely silent when switched on is a different problem: it could be a tripped reset button, a tripped circuit breaker, or in older units, a failed motor that's reached the end of its service life. Disposals typically run 8 to 12 years depending on usage, and a unit that's died after a decade of regular use is usually more cost-effective to replace than to have a motor repaired, since replacement units aren't especially expensive relative to labor.

Leaks are the other common call, and where the leak is located changes the fix. A leak at the top, where the disposal connects to the sink flange, usually means the plumber's putty or gasket seal has failed and needs to be redone. A leak from the bottom of the unit itself, from the housing rather than a connection point, means the disposal has a failed internal seal and needs replacement rather than repair, since that's not a serviceable part.

For new installations, whether you're adding a disposal to a kitchen that's never had one, or upgrading to a higher horsepower unit, we handle the full install. That means mounting the new unit, connecting it to the sink drain and dishwasher line if applicable, and running it through a test cycle with water before we call the job done. If your kitchen doesn't currently have a dedicated switched outlet under the sink for a disposal, that's worth mentioning when you call, since it may need a quick electrical addition alongside the plumbing work.

If a disposal issue is actually a drain clog downstream of the unit rather than the disposal itself, food waste that made it through but is backing up further down the line, that's a job for our drain cleaning service instead. We'll diagnose which one you actually have before quoting either job.

A disposal that vibrates or rattles noticeably more than usual, even when it's grinding normally, often means the mounting assembly under the sink has loosened over time rather than the motor itself failing. We check and re-tighten the mounting ring as part of any disposal service call, since a loose mount left alone tends to work itself looser and can eventually stress the drain connection above it.

Before you call: quick disposal checks

  • Press the red reset button on the bottom of the unit
  • Check the breaker panel for a tripped circuit
  • Never put your hand inside the disposal, use the hex-key wrench socket to clear a jam manually
  • Note whether it hums (jammed) or is completely silent (electrical or motor)
  • Check under the sink for visible water at the top or bottom of the unit
Never reach into a disposal, even when it's switched off. A jam can be cleared safely from underneath with the hex-key wrench socket built into every standard unit.

Common questions

My disposal hums but doesn't spin. Is it broken?

Probably jammed, not broken. Try the reset button and the hex-key wrench socket underneath the unit first. If it still won't spin, call us.

How long do garbage disposals typically last?

Usually 8 to 12 years with regular residential use. A unit that's died around or past that age is usually more cost-effective to replace than repair.

Can you install a disposal where there wasn't one before?

Yes, though it may require adding a switched outlet under the sink if one isn't already there. We'll check during the quote.

What should never go down a disposal?

Bones, fruit pits, fibrous vegetables like celery, coffee grounds, and grease. These are the most common causes of jams and premature motor wear.
