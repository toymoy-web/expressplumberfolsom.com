Hydro Jetting in Folsom, CA

When a cable snake keeps punching through the same clog, hydro jetting clears the whole pipe wall instead, and it's the tool we reach for on recurring main-line problems.

Hydro jetting uses a specialized nozzle and a high-pressure water stream, well beyond what a garden hose or pressure washer produces, to scour the full interior diameter of a drain or sewer line. Where a cable snake punches a channel through a clog and calls it done, a jetter's rotating nozzle sprays water forward and backward as it travels. It cuts through grease buildup, mineral scale, and root intrusion along the entire length of pipe it passes through, not just the spot where the blockage happened to be sitting.

This matters most for lines with a recurring pattern rather than a one-time clog. A kitchen line with years of grease buildup, a main sewer line with root intrusion at multiple joints, or a commercial kitchen's grease trap system that clogs on a predictable schedule are all better served by jetting than by repeated snaking. Snaking clears the immediate blockage while leaving the buildup along the rest of the pipe wall untouched, which is exactly why the same clog tends to come back.

We always inspect before jetting on any line with unknown condition. A camera inspection tells us whether the pipe itself is sound enough for jetting's pressure, since a pipe that's already cracked, severely corroded, or has separated joints can be damaged further by high pressure rather than cleaned by it. On sound pipe, jetting is safe and effective. On compromised pipe, we'll recommend repair first, our sewer line repair page covers what that looks like, rather than jetting a line that needs structural work instead.

Commercial kitchens and multi-unit properties in the Folsom area are a common candidate for scheduled jetting rather than reactive calls. A restaurant grease trap and drain line that's cleared every few months on a set schedule avoids the emergency backup that happens when buildup is left to accumulate until it fully blocks the line, usually at the worst possible time during service hours.

For residential main lines, jetting is typically the right call in a few situations. A home has had more than one clog in the same section within a year or two, a camera inspection shows significant buildup along the pipe wall, or root intrusion has been confirmed and needs clearing before any lining or repair work happens.

Jetting is also a chemical-free way to clear a line, which matters for homes on a septic system or anyone who'd rather not pour a caustic drain cleaner down their pipes repeatedly. Chemical cleaners can also mask a deeper problem temporarily while doing nothing for the underlying buildup, whereas a jetting pass actually removes it from the pipe wall rather than just dissolving a path through the middle of the clog. We run every jetting job with a trained operator controlling pressure at the nozzle, not a fixed setting applied blindly regardless of pipe material or condition, consistent with California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) drainage system requirements.

When hydro jetting is the right tool

  • A main line has clogged more than once in the same section
  • A camera inspection shows grease or scale buildup along the pipe wall
  • Root intrusion has been confirmed at pipe joints
  • A commercial kitchen line needs scheduled preventive clearing
  • A cable snake has repeatedly failed to resolve the same clog

Before you call

Common questions

Is hydro jetting safe for all pipes?

It's safe for pipe in sound structural condition. We run a camera inspection first on any line with unknown condition, since already-damaged pipe can be harmed further by high pressure.

How is hydro jetting different from snaking?

Snaking punches a channel through a clog at one spot. Jetting scours the entire interior wall of the pipe as it travels, clearing buildup along the whole line rather than just the blockage point.

Do commercial kitchens need jetting on a schedule?

Many do, since grease trap and line buildup happens predictably with regular kitchen use. Scheduled jetting prevents the emergency backups that happen when buildup accumulates unchecked.

Will jetting damage older pipe?

It can if the pipe is already compromised, which is why we assess condition with a camera before jetting rather than assuming it's safe for every line.
