Repiping a house is a bigger decision than most plumbing calls, so we treat it that way: we don't recommend a whole-home repipe until we've confirmed it's actually the right fix, not just the most profitable one to sell. The calls that usually end up here start with something smaller. Water pressure that's dropped noticeably over the past year, water that runs slightly brown or rust-colored after a tap has sat unused, or a pinhole leak that turns out to be one of several once we look closer.

The homes most likely to need this in Folsom are the older ones, particularly in and around the Historic District, where original galvanized steel supply lines are now past their practical lifespan. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, which means the outside of the pipe can look completely fine while the interior diameter has narrowed from decades of mineral and rust buildup. That narrowing is what shows up as falling water pressure, and it gets worse gradually enough that homeowners often don't notice until it's significant.

Newer homes aren't automatically exempt. A house built with an early generation of polybutylene piping, or with copper that was improperly soldered during original construction, can develop leaks well before a galvanized system in an older home would. This is why we inspect before we quote: cutting into a wall or slab to check pipe condition at a couple of representative points tells us far more than assuming based on the house's age alone.

When a repipe is the right call, we typically recommend PEX for most residential work today. It resists the corrosion and scaling that ends galvanized pipe's life, and it has some flex, which makes it more forgiving of ground movement than rigid copper. Copper remains a solid choice in specific situations, particularly for exposed runs or where local code requires it. We'll walk you through the real tradeoffs rather than defaulting to whichever material we happen to have on the truck.

A whole-home repipe is disruptive by nature, wall or slab access is usually required, so we sequence the work to keep at least partial water service running where possible and coordinate drywall patching so you're not left with open walls longer than necessary. If your issue turns out to be isolated to one or two problem sections rather than the whole system, we'll say so and scope a partial repipe instead of selling you a bigger job than the house actually needs.

If the concern that brought you here is a single under-slab leak rather than pipe condition sitewide, it's worth starting with our leak detection and slab leak repair page instead, since a localized leak doesn't always mean the whole system needs replacing. We'll always tell you honestly which situation you're actually in before recommending the bigger job. Every repipe is installed to California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) material and support-spacing requirements.

Repiping in Folsom's older homes

Repiping calls cluster in the oldest part of Folsom, around Sutter Street, the Powerhouse, and the Rainbow Bridge, where homes and small buildings date back many decades. They also come from 1990s neighborhoods such as Natoma Station, Prairie Oaks, and Broadstone as original supply lines approach 30 years old. Where the pipe material is unknown, we inspect first and recommend a partial or full repipe only if the pipe's condition calls for it.

When callers ask about replacing the whole house

Many repiping conversations begin after the third or fourth leak. A homeowner is tired of repairs, the water has started to look rusty, or the pressure has dropped. Some owners have a clear plan, such as running new lines through the attic instead of through the slab.

We explain the difference between spot repair and repiping so you can decide what makes sense for your home. The City of Folsom's pinhole leaks page is worth reading if you have copper pipe, since it reports that a majority of reported leaks occurred on cold water lines.

Signs your home may need repiping

  • Water pressure has dropped noticeably over months or years
  • Rust-colored or discolored water, especially after taps sit unused
  • Recurring pinhole leaks in different spots on the same supply line
  • Visible corrosion or flaking on exposed galvanized pipe
  • Home has original galvanized or early polybutylene piping

Common questions

How do I know if I actually need a full repipe?

We inspect first, cutting into a wall or two to check pipe condition at representative points, before recommending a whole-home repipe. Sometimes the fix is a partial repipe of the worst sections instead.

PEX or copper for a Folsom home?

PEX is our usual recommendation for residential repiping, it resists corrosion and handles ground movement better than rigid copper. Copper still makes sense for certain exposed runs or where code requires it.

How long does a whole-home repipe take?

It varies with house size and access, but most single-family homes take a few days. We sequence the work to keep partial water service running where we can.

Will you patch the walls after repiping?

We coordinate drywall access and patching as part of the job so you're not left with open walls longer than the plumbing work requires.

Can you repipe through the attic instead of the slab?

Often, yes. Rerouting through the attic can avoid opening floors, depending on your home's layout. We look at the house before we recommend a route.
