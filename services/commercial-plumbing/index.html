Commercial plumbing work differs from residential in ways that go beyond scale. Backflow prevention devices, required on most commercial properties to protect the public water supply from potential contamination, need annual testing and certification by a licensed tester, a compliance requirement most residential customers never encounter. Grease traps and interceptors in any food service business need regular pumping and cleaning on a schedule, since a neglected grease trap doesn't just clog eventually, it can trigger a city violation and a costly emergency shutdown during business hours.

Multi-fixture commercial restrooms see a different failure pattern than a single-family home. Higher-cycle commercial-grade fixtures still wear out, but the bigger issue is usually a main line or branch line undersized or aged relative to daily use, especially in older Folsom commercial buildings that weren't originally built for their current tenant's foot traffic. We assess the actual line capacity rather than just patching the symptom when a commercial restroom has recurring clog or pressure issues.

Scheduling around business hours matters as much as the technical work itself. A restaurant can't have its kitchen offline during service hours for a repair that could happen before opening or after close, and a retail space needs restrooms functional during business hours, period. We work with property managers and business owners directly to schedule non-emergency work around actual operating hours, and we treat true commercial emergencies, a main line backup during business hours, a burst pipe threatening inventory or equipment, with the urgency they require.

Water heater capacity is a common oversight in commercial spaces that have changed use over time. A building originally set up as a small office with a standard residential-sized water heater sometimes gets converted to a use with much higher hot water demand, a salon, a gym, a daycare, without the capacity ever being reassessed. If your business is running out of hot water regularly, that's often a sizing mismatch from before your tenancy rather than a failing unit.

For property managers overseeing multiple units or a mixed-use building, we can set up a standing maintenance relationship rather than reactive one-off calls, covering backflow testing renewals, scheduled grease trap service, and a faster response commitment for tenant plumbing issues across the property.

True commercial emergencies get a different response than a scheduled maintenance call, and we treat them that way. A main line backup affecting multiple tenants, a burst pipe threatening inventory or server equipment, or a gas smell in a commercial kitchen all warrant immediate dispatch regardless of time of day. That's the same urgency we'd apply to a residential emergency, just scaled to the higher stakes a business interruption usually carries. If you manage a property where downtime translates directly into lost revenue, it's worth having our number on file before an emergency happens rather than searching for a plumber mid-crisis while a kitchen or restroom sits offline.

Common questions

Do you provide backflow testing and certification?

Yes, annual backflow testing is a compliance requirement for most commercial properties, and we handle testing and certification through a licensed tester.

How often does a grease trap need pumping?

It depends on kitchen volume, but most food service businesses need it on a monthly to quarterly schedule to avoid a violation or emergency shutdown.

Can you work around our business hours?

Yes, we schedule non-emergency commercial work before or after operating hours whenever possible, and treat genuine emergencies with immediate priority regardless of time.

Do you work with property managers on multiple units?

Yes, we can set up a standing maintenance relationship covering backflow renewals, scheduled service, and faster response commitments across a property.
