A water heater repair call almost always comes down to one of a handful of real issues, and most of them are fixable without replacing the whole unit. A gas unit with a pilot light that won't stay lit is usually a thermocouple problem, a small, inexpensive part that fails long before the tank itself does. An electric unit that's suddenly producing lukewarm water instead of hot is often a failed heating element or a tripped high-limit switch, not a dying tank, and both are straightforward parts to source and replace.

Other repairs are just as common but slightly more involved. A leaking fitting or valve at the top of the tank, where the cold inlet and hot outlet connect, can usually be repaired or replaced without touching the tank itself. A pressure relief valve that's dripping is doing its job (releasing excess pressure) but a valve that won't stop dripping even after the pressure normalizes usually needs replacing rather than tightening further.

The honest question we answer on every repair call is whether the fix is worth doing at all given the unit's age. A five-year-old tank with a failed thermocouple is an easy repair call. A twelve-year-old tank with the same symptom is a different conversation. You're likely to be back here again within a year or two for something else as the tank continues to age, and at that point the repair cost starts competing directly with a straightforward replacement.

We also see water heater problems that turn out not to be about the water heater at all. Inconsistent hot water across different fixtures in the house sometimes traces back to a failing mixing valve or a plumbing layout issue rather than the tank. A rotten-egg smell from hot water taps specifically, not cold, is a bacterial reaction with the anode rod, fixable with a rod replacement and a tank flush rather than a full unit swap.

If your unit is still under manufacturer warranty, we'll check that before recommending a paid repair, since some parts and even labor can be covered depending on the unit's age and the original installer's paperwork. And if a leaking or failed unit has already caused water damage to the surrounding floor or drywall, that cleanup is separate from the repair itself. Our water heater installation and replacement page covers what a full swap looks like if repair isn't the right call for your unit.

Same-day repair is realistic for most of the issues listed above, a thermocouple, heating element, or valve replacement doesn't typically require a special-order part, so we carry the common failure parts on the truck rather than scheduling a separate visit once parts arrive. Every repair is performed to California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards. If your unit uses an uncommon or discontinued part, we'll tell you honestly at diagnosis time rather than after a wasted trip, and we'll give you a realistic timeline for sourcing it rather than an open-ended guess about when it might actually show up on our end.

Common water heater repair issues

  • Pilot light won't stay lit (gas units)
  • Lukewarm water only, or hot water runs out fast (often a heating element)
  • Dripping from the pressure relief valve or a supply fitting
  • Rotten-egg smell from hot water taps specifically
  • Rumbling or popping sounds (sediment buildup)

Before you call

Common questions

Should I repair or replace my water heater?

It depends on the unit's age and the specific failure. A unit under 8 years old with an isolated issue is usually worth repairing. Beyond that, we'll walk through the real cost comparison so you can decide.

Why does my hot water smell like rotten eggs?

Usually a reaction between the anode rod and sulfur-reducing bacteria in the water. A rod replacement and tank flush typically resolves it.

Is a leaking pressure relief valve dangerous?

A valve that opens briefly and closes is functioning correctly. One that won't stop dripping needs replacement and shouldn't be ignored, since it can indicate excess tank pressure.

Is my repair covered under warranty?

Possibly, depending on the unit's age and manufacturer. We'll check before recommending a paid repair.
