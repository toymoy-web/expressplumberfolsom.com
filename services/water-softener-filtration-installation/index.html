Water Softener & Filtration Installation in Folsom, CA

Folsom's water carries enough mineral content to leave real marks on appliances and fixtures over time. A softener or filtration system addresses that at the source.

The clearest signs of hard water usually show up in places you'd expect: white scale buildup around faucet aerators and showerheads, a filmy residue on glassware straight out of the dishwasher, and soap that doesn't lather as easily as it should. Less obvious is what the same minerals are doing inside your plumbing system and appliances, gradually narrowing pipe diameter, shortening a water heater's useful life, and reducing the efficiency of anything that heats water, from the dishwasher to the washing machine.

A water softener addresses this through ion exchange, swapping the calcium and magnesium minerals responsible for hardness with sodium or potassium ions as water passes through a resin tank. The system needs periodic regeneration, either on a timer or triggered by actual water usage, and periodic salt refills, which is the main ongoing maintenance a softener requires once installed.

Whole-home filtration is a related but distinct upgrade, and the two are sometimes confused. A softener targets hardness minerals specifically. A filtration system targets other things entirely: chlorine taste and odor, sediment, and in some cases specific contaminants depending on the filter media used. Many homes benefit from both installed together, a softener protecting the plumbing and appliances, filtration improving what actually comes out of the tap for drinking and cooking.

Sizing matters more than most homeowners expect going in. A softener sized for a two-person household won't keep up with a family of five running multiple showers and a dishwasher daily, and it will regenerate far more often than it should, burning through salt faster and wearing the resin bed down sooner. We size based on household size, water hardness level, and actual usage patterns rather than defaulting to a mid-range unit for every install.

Installation involves tying into the main water line, typically right after it enters the house and before it branches to the water heater, so the whole home benefits rather than just select fixtures. We also confirm whether a bypass valve is properly installed, since that lets you route around the softener entirely for outdoor irrigation or hose bibs where softened water isn't necessary and just wastes salt and capacity.

For drinking water specifically, a dedicated under-sink reverse osmosis system is worth considering alongside or instead of whole-home filtration. Reverse osmosis removes a broader range of dissolved solids than a standard whole-home filter, delivering noticeably cleaner-tasting water right at the kitchen tap. It's not intended to treat the whole house's water supply the way a whole-home system or softener does, so we install both configurations and can walk through which combination actually fits your household's priorities, whether that's protecting appliances, improving whole-house water quality, or simply having better-tasting drinking water at the kitchen sink every day.

Signs your water needs treatment

  • White scale buildup on faucets, showerheads, or glassware
  • Soap and shampoo don't lather easily
  • Water heater is scaling up faster than expected
  • Noticeable chlorine taste or smell from the tap
  • Spots or film on dishes even after a full wash cycle
  • Skin or hair feels dry after showering despite no change in products
  • Ice cubes come out cloudy or with visible sediment
  • Faucet finish looks dull or corroded well before it should

Before you call

Common questions

Do I need a softener, a filter, or both?

A softener addresses hardness minerals specifically. A filter addresses taste, odor, and sediment. Many Folsom homes benefit from both, we can assess your water and household needs to recommend the right combination.

How much maintenance does a water softener need?

Mainly periodic salt refills and occasional resin bed checks. Most systems regenerate automatically on a schedule or usage trigger with minimal hands-on attention required.

Will a water softener make my water taste salty?

No. The ion exchange process replaces hardness minerals with a small amount of sodium, but it's not enough to be noticeable in taste for most people.

What size system do I need for my household?

It depends on household size, your water's actual hardness level, and daily usage patterns. We size on-site rather than recommending a one-size-fits-all unit.
