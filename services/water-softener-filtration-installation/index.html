Start with the water itself. The City of Folsom draws all of its drinking water from Folsom Lake, and the city's 2023 Water Quality Report lists total dissolved solids at roughly 49 to 65 parts per million. That is a low mineral level, which means Folsom city water is generally soft. For most homes on city water, a whole-house softener solves a problem that is not there.

That is why we test before we sell anything. A simple hardness test takes a few minutes and tells us whether a softener would change anything in your house. If the number is low, we will say so and save you the cost. If the number is high, which can happen on a private well or in a home served by a different water supplier, we will size a system to the real reading.

Softening is not the only kind of treatment. Many Folsom homeowners are really after better taste or less chlorine smell, and that is a filtration question rather than a hardness question. A carbon filter at the kitchen sink or on the main line reduces taste and odor. A reverse osmosis system gives the cleanest drinking water at the kitchen tap. We install both.

A softener works through ion exchange. Water passes through a resin bed that swaps calcium and magnesium for sodium or potassium, and the system regenerates periodically and needs salt refills. Where hardness is high, that is a fair trade. Where hardness is already low, you would be paying for salt and maintenance with little to show for it.

Sizing matters when treatment is warranted. A unit sized for two people will not keep up with a family of five, and an undersized softener regenerates too often and wastes salt. We size from the household's size, the measured hardness, and real usage patterns. We also confirm a bypass valve is installed, so outdoor taps and irrigation do not use up softened water.

White spots on glassware or crust on a showerhead are a useful clue, but they do not prove hard water on their own. Rinse aid settings, water heater temperature, and the age of your fixtures all play a part, so we measure rather than guess. Measuring first is also how we avoid selling a system that does nothing for you.

If your home is on a private well, or if you live in a nearby community served by a different supplier than the City of Folsom, your water may differ. Ask us for a test and we will tell you what we find, including when the answer is that you need nothing. Every installation follows California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards.

Water testing across Folsom

We can test water in any Folsom neighborhood, from Historic Folsom to Empire Ranch, Broadstone, Natoma Station, and the Folsom Plan Area. The city's 2023 report shows similarly low mineral levels in both of its water systems, so we expect soft readings across town. A test confirms that for your tap, and it also catches anything specific to your house, such as an old filter or a point-of-use device.

What to do before buying a water treatment system

  • Get a hardness reading first, with test strips or a lab report
  • Find out whether you are on city water or a private well
  • Compare the reading to the soft range, roughly under 60 ppm
  • Decide whether your real complaint is taste or odor, which is a filtration question
  • Have any system sized to measured hardness and household size
Not sure what is in your tap water? Ask for a test first. A few minutes with a meter is cheaper than a system you do not need.

Before you call

Common questions

Does Folsom have hard water?

City of Folsom tap water is generally soft. The city's 2023 Water Quality Report lists total dissolved solids at roughly 49 to 65 ppm, which is a low mineral level. Homes on a private well or a different supplier can differ, so we test first.

Do I need a water softener in Folsom?

Usually not on city water. We test your water first and only recommend a softener if the reading shows it would help.

What can filtration do that a softener cannot?

Filtration addresses taste, chlorine odor, and sediment, while a softener only removes hardness minerals. Reverse osmosis at the kitchen tap gives the cleanest drinking water.

How long does a water test take?

A basic hardness reading takes a few minutes during a visit. A certified lab test is available when you want a more detailed report.
