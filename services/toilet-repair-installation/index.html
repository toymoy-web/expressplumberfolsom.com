A toilet that runs continuously, or cycles on by itself every few minutes, is one of the most common calls we get. It's almost always one of two parts: a worn flapper that no longer seals the flush valve, or a fill valve that isn't shutting off cleanly once the tank refills. Both are inexpensive parts to replace, but a running toilet left unaddressed can waste a genuinely significant amount of water over a billing cycle, so it's worth fixing quickly even though it feels minor.

A toilet that rocks or wobbles at the base usually points to a failed wax ring, the seal between the toilet base and the drain flange below it. This isn't just an annoyance: a failed wax ring seal can let water seep down into the subfloor over time, which is a much bigger and more expensive problem if it goes unnoticed for months. If your toilet has started rocking recently, that's worth addressing before it becomes a flooring repair on top of a toilet repair.

Clogs that a plunger won't clear sometimes mean an object is lodged past the trap, and repeatedly plunging without success can occasionally push the clog further in rather than clearing it. We use a closet auger, a tool specifically designed for toilet drain lines, for clogs that don't respond to a standard plunger. We'll also check whether the issue is isolated to that fixture or part of a larger drain pattern worth a look at our drain cleaning service.

For installations, whether you're replacing an old water-hungry toilet, upgrading to a comfort-height bowl, or need a new unit set during a bathroom remodel, we handle the full job. That means removing the old unit, resetting the wax ring and flange, and confirming the new toilet is level and properly secured before we consider the job done. A poorly set installation is exactly what causes the rocking and leaking problems described above, so we don't rush this part.

If you're dealing with a specific flange issue, a broken or corroded flange, or one that sits too low after a flooring upgrade, mention it when you call. It changes the repair approach from a straightforward wax ring swap to a flange repair or extender kit first.

Dual-flush and other water-saving toilets are worth considering during any replacement, since older toilets can use significantly more water per flush than a modern high-efficiency unit, and the difference shows up directly on a water bill over a year of regular use. We also install comfort-height and ADA-compliant toilets for homeowners planning to age in place or accommodating a family member with mobility needs. These sit a few inches taller than a standard toilet, which makes a real difference for anyone who has difficulty with a lower seat.

Every installation we perform is done to California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards, with the correct rough-in measurement confirmed carefully before the new unit is set, not assumed from the old one. Rough-in distances can vary even between toilets that look nearly identical at a glance.

Overflowing and Leaking Toilet Emergencies

A toilet that will not stop overflowing, leaks at the base, or has stopped flushing in the only bathroom is an emergency in most households. The water on the floor can reach the subfloor and the room below quickly.

We handle these calls around the clock. We stop the leak, clear or replace what failed, and check the supply line and shutoff valve so the next problem does not sit behind this one.

If the toilet is overflowing

  • Lift the tank lid and push the flapper down, or close the shutoff valve behind the toilet by turning it clockwise.
  • Do not flush again. Use towels to keep water off the floor.
  • If water is reaching a lower floor, shut off the main valve.
  • Tell us whether it is a single toilet or several fixtures, because that decides whether this is a fixture problem or a line problem.

Toilets that will not flush, keep running, or leak

Toilet calls are among the most common we get. Weak flushes, a handle that does nothing, a tank that runs all night, and a puddle at the base all trace to a small number of parts. The flapper, fill valve, flush lever, and wax ring account for most of them.

Running toilets deserve attention even when they seem harmless. A toilet that runs on and off wastes water all day and is a common reason a water bill climbs. We bring the common parts so most calls are repaired in one visit.

Replacing a toilet and fixing a clog that will not clear

If a toilet is cracked, loose on its flange, or constantly clogging, replacement is usually the right call. We handle the removal, flange check, and hookup. When more than one toilet or fixture backs up, we look at the drain line and not the fixture itself.

Signs your toilet needs attention

  • Toilet runs continuously or cycles on by itself
  • Toilet rocks or wobbles at the base
  • Water seeping out at the base of the toilet
  • Repeated clogs that a plunger won't clear
  • Toilet is original to an older home and constantly needs adjustment

Common questions

Can you repair an overflowing toilet at night?

Yes. Our emergency line is open 24/7. Close the shutoff valve behind the toilet if you can, and call. If more than one fixture is backing up, tell us because the cause may be the main line.

Why does my toilet keep running?

Almost always a worn flapper or a fill valve that isn't shutting off completely. Both are quick, inexpensive fixes.

Is a wobbly toilet a serious problem?

It can become one. A wobble usually means the wax ring seal has failed, and a failed seal can let water damage the subfloor over time if it's not addressed.

Can you install a specific toilet brand I've already bought?

Yes, we install customer-supplied toilets as well as units we provide. We'll confirm the rough-in measurement matches before the appointment.

How long does a toilet installation take?

Typically under two hours for a standard swap with no flange or flooring complications.

Can you repair a one-piece toilet or a broken flush lever?

Yes. One-piece toilets use different parts than two-piece models, so tell us the brand if you know it. A broken flush lever is often a quick, inexpensive repair.
