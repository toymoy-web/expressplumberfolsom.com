Hydro Jetting vs. Snaking: Which Do You Need?

They solve different problems. Using the wrong one either wastes money or leaves the real issue unaddressed.

By the Folsom Express Plumbing team

Snaking and hydro jetting both clear clogged drains, but they work in fundamentally different ways, and using the wrong one for your situation either overpays for a simple fix or underdelivers on a bigger problem. Understanding the real difference helps you know what to expect, and what to ask for, when you call about a clog.

A cable snake works mechanically: a flexible cable with a cutting or corkscrew head at the end is fed through the pipe until it reaches the clog, then it either breaks through the blockage or hooks it and pulls it back out. This is fast, effective, and inexpensive for a localized clog, hair in a bathroom drain, grease near a kitchen sink trap, a single foreign object lodged in a toilet's trap. For these situations, snaking clears the immediate problem completely, and there's no reason to pay for anything more involved.

Hydro jetting works differently. A specialized nozzle sprays water at very high pressure, well beyond a garden hose or pressure washer, through the pipe. The nozzle's design sprays both forward and backward as it travels, scouring the entire interior wall of the pipe rather than just punching through the clog at one point. This matters for a fundamentally different category of problem. A main line with years of grease buildup gradually narrowing the pipe's effective diameter, root intrusion at multiple points along a sewer lateral, or a commercial kitchen line that clogs on a predictable schedule due to ongoing grease accumulation are all examples.

The clearest signal that you need jetting rather than snaking is a repeat pattern: the same drain or main line clogging again within weeks or a few months of being snaked clear. Snaking only clears the specific point where the cable made contact. If the underlying cause is buildup along a longer stretch of pipe, or root intrusion at more than one joint, snaking will keep providing temporary relief without ever addressing why the problem keeps coming back.

Cost is part of the honest comparison too. Snaking is generally less expensive than jetting, and for a genuinely isolated clog, paying for jetting would be paying for more than the situation actually requires. But repeatedly paying for snaking visits every few months on a line that actually needs jetting ends up costing more over a year or two than just addressing it properly the first time. There's also the ongoing inconvenience of a drain that keeps backing up.

Pipe condition is the other factor that determines which tool is appropriate, and it's not something you can assess from the outside. A camera inspection before jetting confirms the pipe is structurally sound enough to handle the water pressure involved. Jetting a line that's already cracked, severely corroded, or has separated joints can worsen existing damage rather than clean it, which is exactly why we don't jet blindly on a line with unknown condition, we check first.

If you're not sure which situation you're in, describing the pattern when you call helps. Telling us whether it's the first time this drain has clogged, or whether it's happened multiple times, lets us bring the right equipment on the first visit. That avoids a second trip when snaking proves insufficient for a problem that actually needed jetting from the start.

Commercial properties often benefit from a scheduled jetting approach rather than waiting for a clog to happen at all. A restaurant kitchen line, for instance, accumulates grease on a predictable timeline tied to cooking volume, and jetting that line on a set quarterly or monthly schedule, depending on volume, prevents the kind of sudden backup that can shut down a kitchen during service. This is a different mindset than residential drain care, where reactive snaking for an occasional clog is usually perfectly reasonable, since a typical home doesn't generate grease and food waste at anywhere near a commercial kitchen's volume.

Whichever tool your situation calls for, the work should always start with an honest diagnosis rather than defaulting to whichever service happens to be more profitable to sell. We'll tell you plainly if a simple snake will solve your problem, even though jetting costs more. A customer who gets the right fix the first time is more likely to call us again than one who feels oversold on a service they didn't actually need.

It's also worth knowing that jetting and snaking aren't always an either-or choice for a given job. In some cases, particularly on a line where a camera inspection reveals both an immediate obstruction and broader buildup along the pipe, the right approach is snaking first to restore flow quickly, followed by a jetting pass to address the underlying buildup properly. We'll explain the reasoning behind a combined approach when it applies rather than defaulting to one tool alone just because it's simpler to describe.

Equipment access is a practical factor worth mentioning as well. Hydro jetting requires bringing specialized truck-mounted or portable jetting equipment to the property, which is why it's typically scheduled rather than handled as a same-visit add-on the way an unplanned snake sometimes can be if we're already on-site for something else. If a camera inspection during a snaking visit reveals a jetting-level problem, we'll usually schedule that as a follow-up appointment with the right equipment rather than trying to force an on-the-spot fix that the situation doesn't actually support. Any resulting repair work follows California Plumbing Code (UPC-based) standards.

When snaking is enough vs. when jetting is worth it

  • Snaking: a single, isolated clog in one fixture's drain line
  • Snaking: a clog that hasn't recurred after being cleared before
  • Jetting: a main line or sewer lateral with recurring clogs
  • Jetting: confirmed root intrusion or significant grease buildup along the pipe
  • Jetting: a commercial kitchen line needing scheduled preventive clearing

Why repeat clogs happen, and what the City sees

The City of Folsom's wastewater division says blockages from grease and large debris are its top maintenance issues. Crews inspect and clean the sanitary sewer mains regularly, but the pipe from your house to the main is a separate line.

If you have a repeat clog and are not sure whether the problem is on your side or the City's, call the wastewater division at 916-461-6177. A camera inspection of your own line can show what is causing the repeat clogs and whether snaking or jetting is the better tool.

We also serve nearby Granite Bay and Citrus Heights, so help is close if you are in one of those areas.
